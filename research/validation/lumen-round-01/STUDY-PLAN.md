# LUMEN — Validation Round 01

**Evidence state:** `PLANNED_VALIDATION`  
**Study type:** moderated discovery/orientation usability test  
**Target:** 5 participants matching weak-intent art/culture exploration behavior; optionally include at least one keyboard/reduced-motion-focused accessibility session if recruitment allows.

## Product hypothesis under test

If visual/semantic discovery is organized around **mood, color, era, movement and relationships** rather than only search/category grids, people with weak intent may be able to discover meaningful artworks with less cognitive friction and stronger orientation.

This is a hypothesis, not an outcome.

## Decision map

- `D-01` — Parallel discovery modes (Drift / Grid / Color / Mood) instead of a single archive browse model.
- `D-02` — Relationship Atlas as a continuation path after artwork detail.
- `D-03` — Motion as orientation/connection rather than decoration, with a reduced-motion semantic fallback.
- `D-04` — Saved Collection as a lightweight return point rather than forcing account-heavy collection management.

## Tasks

### Task 1 — Discover without a known target
Prompt: “You do not have a specific artist in mind. Find an artwork that feels tense, restless or emotionally charged. Use whatever path makes sense to you.”

Observe:
- which discovery mode is chosen and why;
- whether labels/modes are understood before entering;
- time to first meaningful artwork;
- whether participant falls back to search because discovery is unclear.

### Task 2 — Continue through a relationship
Prompt: “From this artwork, find another work that feels meaningfully connected. Tell me what you think the connection is.”

Observe Relationship Atlas comprehension, connection semantics, orientation and whether the user knows how to continue/return.

### Task 3 — Save and return
Prompt: “Save something you want to revisit, then leave the artwork and find it again.”

Observe saved-state feedback, collection findability, empty/populated behavior and return path.

### Task 4 — Reduced-motion / keyboard path
Enable reduced motion or use the semantic/keyboard path.
Prompt: “Repeat a short discovery journey without relying on cinematic motion.”

Observe whether content/function remains equivalent, focus order, spatial/orientation cues and whether motion removal creates missing meaning.

## Measures

For each task record:
- outcome: success / partial / failure;
- time on task (approximate);
- navigation/reorientation errors;
- selected discovery mode;
- moderator assistance: none / light / direct;
- confidence/ease: 1–5;
- evidence of mode/relationship comprehension;
- observable accessibility blockers.

Do not turn “interesting” or aesthetic preference into usability success.

## Participant criteria

Good participants may include:
- people who visit museums/galleries or browse visual-culture/art content occasionally;
- design/art students or visually curious general users;
- people who can articulate how they choose what to explore without needing art-history expertise.

Avoid recruiting only designers who already know the interaction concept; peer critique is not the same as target-user evidence.

## Moderator rules

- Do not explain what Drift/Color/Mood/Atlas mean before the participant encounters them.
- Ask “What do you think this will do?” before a click when useful.
- Record behavior first; interpretation second.
- Preserve disagreement: different discovery preferences are expected.
- Do not infer emotional response from facial expression alone.

## Claim boundary

Until real sessions exist, valid wording is only: **“LUMEN has a working multi-mode cultural prototype and a prepared validation round; direct-user discovery outcomes are not measured.”**
